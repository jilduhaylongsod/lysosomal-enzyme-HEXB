## Brief function description

HEXB is a gene that provides instructions for making the beta subunit of an enzyme called beta-hexosaminidase. This enzyme is found in lysosomes and helps break down certain complex sugars and fats so that the cell can recycle them.

## Questions and Answers

**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?**

HEXB produces the beta subunit of beta-hexosaminidase. This enzyme helps break down certain complex sugars and glycolipids inside lysosomes so the cell can recycle them.

**2. What is the total length of your protein, and which amino acids are most common?**

Based on my R analysis, the HEXB protein sequence contains 556 amino acids. I used R to count the amino acids and calculate their percentages. The most common amino acid is leucine (L), with about 63 occurrences. The least common amino acid is cysteine (C), with about 8 occurrences.

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find? Where are they located?**

I found 5 potential N-glycosylation motifs in the HEXB protein sequence. These motifs follow the N-X-S/T pattern, where X cannot be proline. Their positions and sequences are 84 (NST), 142 (NIS), 190 (NES), 327 (NTT), and 497 (NLT).

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is
glycosylated?**

Glycosylation can help proteins fold properly, remain stable, and reach the correct location in the cell. However, finding a possible N-glycosylation motif does not prove that glycosylation actually occurs at that site. Further experimental evidence is needed to confirm it.

**5. What could happen in the cell if your enzyme did not work properly?**

If the HEXB-related enzyme does not work properly, certain substances may build up inside lysosomes because they are not broken down correctly. This can affect normal cell function. Changes in the HEXB gene can cause Sandhoff disease, which can affect the nervous system.
